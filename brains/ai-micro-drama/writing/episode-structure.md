# Summary

Each episode is a self-contained micro-story that still pulls the viewer forward. One widely used shape, called the Beat Engine, has four parts for a 60 to 90 second episode.[^filmustage-script]

| Beat | Time | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | 0 to 15 s | Open on impact, no easing in |
| Friction | 15 to 60 s | Visible, filmable conflict in frame |
| Spike | 60 to 90 s | The biggest jolt: a reversal, new evidence, a POV flip |
| Button | last 5 to 10 s | End on a question, not an answer |

# Key points

- Draft a timestamp skeleton before writing any dialogue.[^filmustage-script]
- A simpler version of the same idea: cold open, escalation, cliffhanger, and nothing else. No B-plot, no slow build.[^invideo-hooks]
- Three questions decide every script choice: what is the hook, what is the reversal, what is the hang.[^invideo-hooks]
- Test the spike with the sound off. If it disappears, it was only volume.[^filmustage-script]
